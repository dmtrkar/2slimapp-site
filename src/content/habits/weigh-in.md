A single weigh-in can go up or down for a dozen reasons that have nothing to do with progress — water, salt, sleep, the time of day. Weighed in isolation, that number is easy to read as good or bad news. Weighed every day and looked at as a trend, it stops being either.

## Feedback, not judgment

The fifth habit is simply stepping on the scale each morning and logging what it says — no interpretation required in the moment. One day's number barely matters on its own. It's the line across a week or a month that tells you something real, smoothed past the daily noise that makes a single reading so easy to over-read.

## Why daily beats occasional

Weighing in once a week can make ordinary day-to-day fluctuation look like a verdict. Weighing in daily does the opposite — it dilutes any single number into part of a pattern, so what you're left looking at is your real trend, not one data point you happened to catch on a bad day.
